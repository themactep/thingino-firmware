Training Pool

Opt in, and know what you are sharing

Thingino is building its own open object-recognition engine. If you turn this on, one JPEG frame from each accepted motion event is sent to the Thingino training pool and used only to train those models.

What is sent:

  • the camera frame itself, with EXIF and other metadata removed by the collector;
  • a random contributor ID generated on this camera. It is a pseudonym that lets the collector count frames per device. It cannot be traced back to you, and it is not derived from your hardware.

What is never sent:

  • your camera's hostname, MAC address, serial number or model;
  • audio, video clips, other files, or your notification settings;
  • your location, account, or any other service credential.
Contribution

Turning this off stops all uploads immediately. The switch on the Services page controls whether frames are sent automatically on motion; this switch is the opt-in itself.

Limits

1 to 48. Counted per UTC day on this camera.

A random delay is added on top, so a group of cameras does not upload in lockstep.

Leave as it is to use the pool run by the Thingino project. Point it at your own collector to keep the frames in-house; the endpoint must use HTTPS.

Deleting /etc/send2-train.token on the camera gives it a new pseudonym and a clean daily quota.
